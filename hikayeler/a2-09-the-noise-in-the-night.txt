The Noise in the Night
A2

It was two o'clock in the morning when Hana woke up. She heard a noise. It came from the garden.

Scratch, scratch. Then silence. Then scratch, scratch again.

Hana lived alone in a small house at the edge of the village. She was not usually afraid, but tonight the noise made her heart beat fast. She took her phone and went to the window. The garden was dark. She could not see anything.

The next morning, she found that someone had dug holes in her vegetable garden. Her strawberries were gone, and the bin was on its side.

"Maybe it's a thief," said her neighbour, Mr Brown. "Or a fox. Or kids."

The next night, the noise came again. Hana was ready this time. She had a torch and a big umbrella, because she didn't have anything better. She opened the back door very slowly and turned on the torch.

In the middle of the garden, there was a small, round animal, covered in brown spikes. It was eating one of her strawberries. When the light touched it, it stopped and looked at her with tiny black eyes.

It was a hedgehog.

Hana laughed so loudly that the hedgehog rolled into a ball. "So you are my thief," she said.

She read about hedgehogs on the internet. They eat insects and snails, they are good for gardens, and they are in danger in many countries because they have nowhere to live.

So Hana changed her garden. She made a small house for the hedgehog from wood and old leaves. She put out a dish of water every evening. She even made a small hole in her fence, so it could visit her neighbours' gardens too.

She lost a few strawberries every summer after that. But she never slept badly again. When she heard scratch, scratch in the night, she just smiled and said, "Good night, thief."
