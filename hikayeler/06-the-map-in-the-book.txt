The Map in the Book
B1

Selin bought the book for one pound at a second-hand market. It was an old book about birds, with a brown cover and yellow pages. She did not really care about birds; she just liked the smell of old paper.

When she opened it at home, a folded piece of paper fell onto the floor. It was a map, drawn by hand in blue ink. There was a river, a bridge, three trees in a line, and a small cross. Under the cross, someone had written: "For whoever finds this. Dig where the shadows meet at noon."

Selin read the words three times. Then she looked more carefully at the map and realised that she knew the place. The bridge was the old stone bridge near her grandfather's village. She had crossed it many times as a child.

The next weekend, she took the bus to the village with a small shovel in her backpack. Her grandfather laughed when she showed him the map.

"People have been looking for treasure here for fifty years," he said. "Nobody has ever found anything."

"Then I'll be the first," Selin answered.

She found the three trees easily. They were still standing in a line beside the river, older and taller now. She sat on the grass and waited. At exactly twelve o'clock, the shadows of the first and the third tree touched each other on the ground, like two hands.

Selin started to dig. After half an hour, her shovel hit something hard. It was a metal box, brown with rust. Her heart was beating fast as she opened it.

There was no gold inside. There was a photograph of a young man and a young woman standing on the stone bridge, and a letter. The letter said: "If you are reading this, you have patience and curiosity. Those are the only treasures that matter. Please put something of your own in this box and hide it again for the next person."

On the back of the photograph, someone had written two names and a year. One of the names was her grandfather's.

When Selin came back to the house, her grandfather was drinking tea in the garden. She put the photograph on the table in front of him without a word.

For a long time, he did not speak. Then he smiled, and his eyes were wet.

"So she really did it," he said quietly. "Your grandmother always said she would hide our story somewhere safe."

That evening, Selin wrote her own letter, put it in the box with a photograph of herself and her grandfather, and buried it under the three trees. Then she folded the map and put it back into the old book about birds, ready for the next market, and the next curious hand.
