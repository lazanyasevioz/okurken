The Interview
B1

Leyla had prepared for the interview for two weeks. She had read everything about the company, practised her answers in front of the mirror, and bought a new jacket that she couldn't really afford. It was her fourth interview that month. The first three had all ended with the same polite email: "Unfortunately, we have decided to continue with other candidates."

On the morning of the interview, everything went wrong. Her alarm didn't ring. The bus was late. And when she finally ran out of the metro station, it started to rain heavily.

She arrived at the office building with seven minutes to spare, wet and out of breath. In the lift, there was an elderly man holding a large box of papers. When the doors opened, the box slipped from his hands, and hundreds of pages flew across the floor of the corridor.

Leyla looked at her watch. Then she looked at the man, who was slowly trying to kneel down.

"Let me help you," she said.

It took them almost ten minutes to collect all the papers and put them back in order. The man thanked her warmly and walked away down the corridor. Leyla ran to reception.

"I'm so sorry I'm late," she said. "I have an interview at ten with Ms Erdem."

The receptionist smiled. "Please take a seat. They're running a little late too."

Fifteen minutes later, she was invited into a meeting room. Three people were sitting behind a long table. Ms Erdem was in the middle. And on her left sat the elderly man from the lift.

Leyla's face turned red. The interview began, and the questions were difficult. When Ms Erdem asked, "Can you tell us about a time you failed?", Leyla hesitated. Then she decided to be honest.

"This month, I failed three interviews," she said. "I think I tried too hard to be the perfect candidate. I said what I thought people wanted to hear. Today, I'm too tired to do that. So I'll just tell you the truth."

And she did. She talked about her mistakes, what she had learned, and why she really wanted the job.

At the end, the elderly man spoke for the first time. "Do you know why you were late this morning?" he asked.

"Yes," said Leyla quietly. "I'm sorry. I helped someone with some papers."

"I know," he said. "I'm the founder of this company. Those papers were forty years of my work. Everybody else in that corridor walked past me."

Two days later, Leyla received an email. It did not begin with "Unfortunately."
