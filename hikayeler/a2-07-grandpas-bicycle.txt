Grandpa's Bicycle
A2

When Zoe was helping her grandfather clean his garage, she found an old bicycle under a dusty blanket. It was red, with a big silver bell and a basket in front.

"Was this yours, Grandpa?" she asked.

Her grandfather stopped working. He touched the bell gently. "I rode this bicycle every day for twenty years," he said. "I rode it to school, to work, and to your grandmother's house when we were young. I haven't ridden it for a long time."

The bicycle was in a bad condition. The tyres were flat, the chain was brown with rust, and the seat was broken.

"Let's fix it," said Zoe.

Grandpa laughed. "It's too old, Zoe. And so am I."

But Zoe did not listen. Every day after school, she came to the garage. She watched videos on her phone about how to repair bicycles. She cleaned the chain with oil. She bought new tyres with her pocket money. Grandpa pretended that he was not interested, but every afternoon he came into the garage "just to look." Soon he was helping her, and telling her stories about every part of the bicycle.

After three weeks, the red bicycle was ready. It looked almost new.

"You ride it first," said Zoe.

Grandpa looked at the bicycle for a long time. Then he sat on the seat, slowly. He rode to the end of the street, very carefully. Then he rode back, a little faster. When he stopped, he was smiling like a boy.

"I forgot how good this feels," he said.

Now, every Sunday morning, Zoe and her grandfather ride together to the park: Zoe on her blue bicycle, and Grandpa on his old red one, ringing the silver bell at everybody they pass.
