The Girl in Goal
A2

Every day after school, the boys in Defne's street played football in the park. Every day, Defne watched them from the bench. She knew all the rules, and she practised alone at home against the garage door. But nobody ever asked her to play.

One Friday, the boys had a problem. They had an important match on Saturday against the team from the next street, and their goalkeeper, Can, had broken his arm.

"We can't play with ten people," said Emre, the captain. "We need a goalkeeper."

Defne stood up from the bench. "I can play," she said.

The boys looked at each other. Some of them laughed.

"Girls can't play in goal," said one boy.

"Try me," said Defne. "Take five penalties. If you score more than two, I'll go home."

Emre took the first penalty. He kicked the ball hard to the left. Defne jumped and caught it. The second boy kicked it to the right. She stopped it with her foot. The third, the fourth and the fifth penalties all went the same way. Nobody scored.

The park was very quiet.

"OK," said Emre slowly. "You're in the team."

On Saturday, the match was difficult. The other team was big and fast, and they attacked again and again. But Defne was everywhere. She caught high balls, she dived to the ground, and she shouted instructions to the defenders like a real goalkeeper.

In the last minute, the score was one-one. The other team's best player ran towards the goal alone. He kicked the ball as hard as he could. Defne flew through the air and pushed the ball over the bar with her fingertips.

The match finished one-one, but the boys carried Defne on their shoulders like they had won the World Cup.

When Can's arm was better, there was a new problem: the team now had two goalkeepers. So Can moved to defence, and Defne kept the gloves.
