Grandma and the Sea
A1

My grandma is seventy-five years old. She lives in a small town in the mountains. She has a garden, two cats and a lot of books. But she has never seen the sea.

"Is it really blue?" she asks me one day. "Is it really very big?"

"Yes, Grandma," I say. "Let's go and see it."

On Saturday, we take the big red bus. The trip is long: four hours. Grandma looks out of the window all the time. She sees fields, rivers, cows and small villages. She eats an apple and tells me stories about when she was a girl.

At one o'clock, the bus stops. We walk for five minutes, and then we see it.

The sea is very big and very blue. The sun is shining on the water. There are white boats and white birds.

Grandma does not speak. She takes off her shoes and walks into the water. The water is cold, and she laughs like a child.

"Why did I wait seventy-five years?" she says.

We sit on the beach until the evening. We eat ice cream and watch the sun go down.

On the bus home, Grandma sleeps with a small white stone in her hand.
