The Exchange Student
B1

When Yuki arrived in Turkey as an exchange student, she had studied Turkish for one year. She could order food, ask for directions, and introduce herself. She thought she was ready. She was wrong.

Her host family, the Demirs, lived in a busy apartment in İzmir with three children, a grandmother, and a cat that slept on the television. On her first evening, the grandmother put a huge plate of food in front of her: rice, beans, salad, bread, and some kind of stuffed vegetables.

Yuki ate everything, because in Japan it is polite to finish your plate. Immediately, the grandmother smiled and filled her plate again. Yuki was full, but she didn't want to be rude, so she ate it all again. The grandmother looked delighted and gave her a third plate.

Later, the eldest daughter, Ece, explained with a laugh. "In our house, if your plate is empty, it means you're still hungry. You have to leave a little bit of food."

There were many more surprises. When Yuki bowed to say thank you, the little boy bowed back, and then the whole family bowed to each other for a week as a joke. When she took off her shoes at the door, they were happy, because they did the same. But when she refused tea for the third time, the grandmother looked genuinely worried about her health.

The hardest part was the language. In class, she understood the teacher perfectly. But at home, everybody spoke fast, at the same time, and with lots of expressions she had never learned. When the father said that something was "as easy as eating a melon," she went to the kitchen to look for a melon.

At first, Yuki felt tired and lonely. Every evening, she called her mother in Osaka and said that everything was fine. It was not completely true.

Then, one weekend, the grandmother asked Yuki to help her make dolma in the kitchen. The grandmother didn't speak any English or Japanese, and Yuki's Turkish was not good enough for a long conversation. But it didn't matter. The grandmother showed her how to fill the vine leaves with rice, and how to roll them tightly. Yuki's first ones were ugly and fell apart. By the end of the afternoon, they were almost perfect.

The next week, Yuki taught the grandmother how to make onigiri, Japanese rice balls. The grandmother's first ones were too big and full of spices. Everyone agreed they were delicious anyway.

When Yuki left after ten months, the whole family came to the airport. The grandmother cried and gave her a container full of dolma for the plane.

"Remember," said Ece, "leave a little bit, or she'll send you more."

Yuki opened the box on the plane and ate every single one.
