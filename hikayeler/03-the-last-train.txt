The Last Train
B1

Daniel ran into the station at eleven fifty-eight. The last train of the night was leaving at midnight, and he could already hear the doors beginning to close.

He jumped inside just in time. The doors shut behind him with a heavy sigh, and the train started to move.

The carriage was almost empty. A woman in a green coat was reading a newspaper, and a boy was sleeping with his head against the window. Daniel sat down, breathing hard, and checked his phone. The battery was dead.

After a few minutes, he noticed something strange. The train was not stopping. They passed the first station, then the second, then the third. The lights outside became fewer and fewer, until there was only darkness.

"Excuse me," Daniel said to the woman. "Do you know where this train is going?"

She lowered her newspaper and looked at him carefully. "Everyone asks that question on their first night," she said.

"My first night?"

"This train only stops for people who have forgotten something important," she explained. "It takes you back to the place where you lost it."

Daniel laughed nervously. He wanted to say that it was impossible, but the boy by the window had woken up, and he was looking at Daniel with calm, serious eyes.

The train slowed down. Through the window, Daniel saw a small platform with a single yellow lamp. Behind it, there was a house that he recognised immediately. It was his grandmother's house, the one he had not visited in eight years.

The doors opened. Cold air came in, smelling of wood smoke and apples.

"You don't have to get off," the woman said gently. "But the train won't come back for you tonight."

Daniel stood up. His legs were shaking. He remembered the last phone call, the promise he had made, and how easy it had been to say "next month" again and again.

He stepped onto the platform. When he turned around, the train was already disappearing into the dark, and the light in his grandmother's kitchen window was on.
