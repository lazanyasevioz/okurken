The Moon Follows Me
A1

Every Friday evening, Adam and his parents drive to his grandmother's house. The trip is one hour. Adam sits in the back of the car and looks out of the window.

Tonight, the sky is dark and clear. There is a big, round, white moon.

The car goes fast. The trees go past. The houses go past. But the moon is always there, next to the car.

"Mum," says Adam. "The moon is following us."

His mother smiles. "Is it?"

"Yes," says Adam. "Look. We go fast, and the moon goes fast. We stop at the red light, and the moon stops too."

His father laughs. "Maybe the moon likes you," he says.

Adam thinks about this. "Maybe it wants to come to Grandma's house," he says. "Grandma makes very good soup."

At Grandma's house, Adam runs into the garden. He looks up. The moon is there, over the roof.

"See?" he says. "It came with us."

Grandma gives him a bowl of hot soup. Adam eats half of it. Then he takes the bowl to the window and puts it on the windowsill.

"What are you doing?" asks Grandma.

"It is for the moon," says Adam. "It is a long trip. The moon is hungry too."
