The Red Umbrella
A2

Mina found the umbrella on a Monday. It was standing alone at the bus stop, bright red, like a flower in the grey rain.

She looked around. There was nobody there. She waited for ten minutes, but nobody came back for it.

"Maybe someone forgot it," she thought. She did not want to steal it, so she left it there and got on the bus.

On Tuesday, the umbrella was still at the bus stop. On Wednesday, it was there again. On Thursday, Mina wrote a note on a piece of paper: "Is this your umbrella? Please take it home." She tied the note to the handle.

On Friday morning, the note was gone. In its place, there was a new note, written in small, careful letters:

"It is not mine. I thought it was yours."

Mina laughed out loud. An old woman next to her smiled.

That evening, Mina wrote another note: "It is not mine either. Let's share it. Whoever needs it can use it."

From that day, the red umbrella became famous in the neighbourhood. When it rained, people used it to walk to the shop or to the school, and then they brought it back to the bus stop. Children drew pictures and left them near it. Someone gave it a name: Ruby.

In spring, Ruby broke. One of its arms bent in the wind. Mina was sad, but the next day there was a small box at the bus stop. Inside it there were tools, some wire, and a note:

"I can fix umbrellas. Meet me here on Saturday at ten."

On Saturday, Mina went to the bus stop. An old man was waiting there with a cup of coffee in each hand.

"I am the first note," he said. "The careful letters."

They fixed Ruby together. It took one hour and two more cups of coffee. When they finished, the sun came out, and they both laughed, because now nobody needed an umbrella at all.
