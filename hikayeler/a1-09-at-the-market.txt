At the Market
A1

Every Friday, Omar goes to the market with his father. Today, his father is ill. He is in bed with a cold.

"Can you go to the market alone?" his father asks. "Here is a list and twenty pounds."

Omar is nine. He is a little afraid, but he says, "Yes, I can."

The list says:
Six apples. One kilo of tomatoes. Bread. A small chicken. Lemons.

The market is very busy and very loud. People are talking, and the sellers are shouting.

First, Omar buys six green apples. They cost two pounds. Then he buys a kilo of red tomatoes. They cost three pounds. The bread costs one pound.

The chicken is expensive. It costs ten pounds. Omar counts his money. He has four pounds now.

The old lemon seller smiles at him. "Lemons are one pound for three," she says. "How many do you want?"

"Three, please," says Omar.

She puts four lemons in his bag. "One is a present," she says. "For a good boy who helps his father."

At home, Omar makes hot water with lemon and honey for his father.

"You are a good shopper," says his father. "Next Friday, you can come with me and show me the cheap lemons."
