Locked in the Library
B1

Cem had always loved the old city library. It had high ceilings, wooden shelves that went up to the roof, and a quiet that felt almost alive. On Friday evening, he went to the fourth floor to find a book for his history project and sat down in a comfortable chair in the corner.

He only closed his eyes for a moment.

When he woke up, the library was dark. The lights were off, and the only sound was the rain on the windows. He looked at his phone: 10:47 p.m. The library had closed at eight.

He ran down the stairs to the main entrance. The doors were locked. He tried the emergency exit, but it was locked too, with a sign that said "Alarm will sound." He didn't want the police to come, so he didn't touch it. He called his mother, but his phone had no signal inside the thick stone walls.

Cem sat down on the stairs and took a deep breath. He was locked in a library for the whole night. Strangely, after the first shock, he didn't feel afraid. He felt excited.

He used the light from his phone to explore. He walked through the children's section, where he had first learned to love books when he was five. He found the map room, with enormous old maps of cities that no longer existed. In a glass case on the third floor, he saw a handwritten letter from a famous poet, and he read it three times.

At around midnight, he heard a noise. Footsteps, slow and heavy, coming up the stairs. Cem's heart started to beat fast. He hid behind a shelf.

A torch appeared, and behind it an old man in a uniform. It was the night guard. He was humming a song and carrying a thermos.

Cem stepped out. "Excuse me," he said. "I think I'm locked in."

The guard jumped so high that he almost dropped his thermos. Then he started to laugh. "Thirty years I've worked here," he said, "and every few years I find someone like you. Usually they're asleep on the fourth floor."

His name was Mr Rıza. He let Cem use the office phone to call his mother, who was very worried and then very angry. Then, while they waited for her to come, Mr Rıza poured him a cup of tea from his thermos and showed him things that most visitors never saw: the secret room where damaged books were repaired, the original plans of the building, and a book from 1650 with a dried flower still pressed between its pages.

When his mother arrived, Cem didn't really want to leave.

The next week, he went back to the library and asked if they needed volunteers. Now he works there every Saturday. And sometimes, when he finds a student asleep on the fourth floor, he gently wakes them up before the doors close.
