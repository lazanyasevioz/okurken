Lost in the Fog
B1

The weather was perfect when Oscar and his sister Nina started walking up the mountain. The sky was blue, the air was fresh, and the path was easy to follow. They planned to reach the top by midday, eat their sandwiches, and come back down before dark.

They reached the top at twelve thirty. The view was incredible: green valleys, a silver lake and, far away, the roofs of the village where they were staying. They took photos, ate lunch, and lay in the sun for a while.

That was their mistake.

When they started to go back, a thin white cloud was moving up the valley. At first it looked beautiful. Within twenty minutes, it surrounded them completely. They couldn't see more than three metres in front of them. The path disappeared under their feet, and every rock looked the same.

"Let's check the map on your phone," said Nina.

Oscar took out his phone. There was no signal, and the battery was at eleven percent.

They tried to walk down carefully, but after an hour they realised they were going in circles. They passed the same strange rock twice. The temperature dropped, and it started to drizzle. Nina's jacket was not waterproof.

"We should stop," said Oscar finally. "If we keep walking, we might fall."

They sat behind a large rock, out of the wind, and shared the last chocolate bar. Neither of them said what they were both thinking: that nobody knew exactly where they were.

Then they heard a sound. It was a bell, quiet at first, then louder. Out of the fog came a large, wet, black-and-white sheepdog. It had a small bell on its collar. It looked at them, barked once, and walked a few steps away. Then it stopped and looked back.

"I think it wants us to follow," said Nina.

It seemed crazy, but they didn't have a better idea. They followed the dog for almost an hour. Whenever they slowed down, it waited. Whenever they stopped, it barked. Slowly, the path became clearer, and the fog became thinner.

Suddenly they were out of the cloud, standing above a small farm. A woman was standing at the gate with a torch.

"There you are, Bess!" she called to the dog. Then she saw Oscar and Nina, and her expression changed.

"Lost in the fog?" she asked. "You're not the first. She does this every time the cloud comes down. She thinks walkers are just sheep with backpacks."

The woman gave them hot soup and drove them back to the village. Before they left, Nina knelt down and hugged the wet dog.

"Thank you, Bess," she said.

Bess just wagged her tail, as if rescuing people was the most normal job in the world.
