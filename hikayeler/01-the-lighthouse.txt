The Lighthouse
A2

Every evening, old Tom climbed the stairs of the lighthouse. There were one hundred and twelve stairs, and he knew each one. Some of them were cold, some of them were broken, and one of them always made a small sound under his feet.

At the top, he cleaned the big glass and turned on the light. Then he sat by the window with a cup of tea and watched the sea.

For forty years, nothing strange happened. Ships came and went. Birds flew over the waves. In winter, the wind was loud and the rain hit the windows like small stones.

One night in October, Tom saw a tiny boat near the rocks. It had no light. He picked up his old radio and called, but nobody answered.

He did not think for long. He put on his heavy coat, took his lamp, and ran down all one hundred and twelve stairs. His knees hurt, but he did not stop.

On the beach, the waves were high. Tom held the lamp above his head and moved it from left to right, again and again. Slowly, the little boat turned away from the rocks and came towards the light.

Inside the boat, there was a young girl. She was wet and afraid, and she was holding a small dog.

"I lost my way," she said. "The fog came, and I could not see anything."

Tom gave her his coat. "Everybody loses their way sometimes," he said. "That is why we have lighthouses."

They walked up the hill together. The dog walked between them, shaking the water from its fur.

The next morning, the girl's father came to thank him. He brought fresh bread and a jar of honey. Tom did not know what to say, so he made more tea.

After that night, the girl visited the lighthouse every Sunday. She learned the names of the ships and the names of the stars. One day, she asked Tom if she could climb the stairs with him.

"Of course," he said. "But be careful with the fifty-third step. It likes to sing."
