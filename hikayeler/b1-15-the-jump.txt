The Jump
B1

Every summer, the teenagers in Kaya's village had the same tradition. On the last day of the holidays, they jumped from the old stone bridge into the river. It was about six metres high. The water underneath was deep and green, and perfectly safe. Everyone did it. It was how you said goodbye to summer.

Kaya was sixteen, and she had never jumped.

Every year, she walked to the bridge with her friends. Every year, she climbed onto the wall, looked down at the water, and felt her legs turn to stone. Every year, she climbed back down while the others jumped, screaming and laughing, and she told herself: next year.

This year was different. It was the last summer before everything changed. In September, her best friend Ada was moving to Germany with her family. Mehmet was going to a boarding school in the city. Next summer, they would not all be together on the bridge.

"You don't have to do it," Ada said, as they walked to the bridge in the late afternoon. "Nobody cares."

But Kaya cared.

When they arrived, the others jumped one by one. Mehmet did a backflip. Ada jumped holding her nose, as always. Soon, everyone was in the water, shouting and splashing, and Kaya was alone on the bridge.

She climbed onto the wall. The stone was warm from the sun. She looked down, and the water seemed very, very far away. Her heart was beating so hard she could hear it. The old feeling came back: her legs heavy, her hands cold, her mind full of terrible pictures.

"Take your time!" called Mehmet from below.

She didn't move. A minute passed. Then another. Slowly, the others stopped shouting. They just floated in the water, looking up at her, waiting.

Kaya closed her eyes. She thought about all the summers that were behind her and all the summers in front of her that would be different. She thought about Ada in Germany, about the empty seat next to her at school. She realised that she was not really afraid of the water. She was afraid of the end of something.

And she realised that the end was coming anyway, whether she jumped or not.

She opened her eyes, took a deep breath, and stepped off the bridge.

For one long second, there was nothing — only air and sky and the sound of the wind. Then the cold green water closed over her head, and she was under, and then she was up, gasping and laughing, and her friends were shouting and splashing and swimming towards her.

"You did it!" screamed Ada, throwing her arms around her. "You actually did it!"

They stayed in the river until the sun went down. Then they sat on the bridge, wet and cold, eating sunflower seeds and not talking very much.

In September, Ada left for Germany. It was hard, but Kaya was ready. Some things end, she thought. That's what makes them worth jumping for.
