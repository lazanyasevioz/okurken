The Snow Day
A2

Nobody expected the snow. The weather forecast said "light rain," but in the night, the rain became snow, and it didn't stop.

When people in Maple Street woke up, the city was white and silent. The snow was almost one metre deep. There were no buses, the schools were closed, and the cars were under big white blankets. The electricity worked, but the internet was down.

At first, everybody stayed inside. But after a few hours without phones and computers, people started to get bored. One by one, they came out of their houses.

Mr and Mrs Park, who lived at number 3, came out with shovels and started to clear the street. The students from number 8 joined them. Mrs Garcia, who was eighty, couldn't help with the snow, so she made a huge pot of soup and gave a bowl to everybody.

The children built a snowman as tall as a door. Then they had a snowball fight, and soon the adults joined in too. Mr Park, who was a serious bank manager, got a snowball in the face and laughed until he fell down.

In the afternoon, someone brought out a guitar. People sat on the steps of number 5, drinking hot tea and singing old songs. The family at number 10, who had lived on the street for three years, realised that they didn't know the names of any of their neighbours. By the evening, they knew everybody.

The next day, the snow began to melt. The buses came back, the schools opened, and the internet worked again. Life went back to normal.

But not completely. Now, the people of Maple Street say hello to each other in the morning. And every year on the same date, they have a street party with soup, music and hot tea.

They call it "Snow Day," even when there isn't any snow.
