The Parrot Who Talked
A2

When Aunt Selma went to Australia for a month, she left her parrot, Paşa, with the Yılmaz family. Paşa was green and yellow, he was forty years old, and he could say more than a hundred words.

At first, the family loved him. In the morning, he said "Good morning!" When someone came home, he said "Welcome!" and when the phone rang, he said "Hello? Hello?" in Aunt Selma's voice.

But Paşa also listened. All day, he sat in his cage in the living room and learned new words.

On Saturday, the family invited their new neighbours, Mr and Mrs Kaya, for dinner. Everybody was very polite. Mrs Yılmaz made her best dishes, and Mr Yılmaz wore his best shirt.

In the middle of dinner, Mrs Kaya said, "What a beautiful parrot! Can he talk?"

"Oh yes," said Mrs Yılmaz proudly. "Say something, Paşa."

Paşa looked at the guests. Then he said, in Mr Yılmaz's voice: "The new neighbours are coming? Oh no. That man talks too much!"

The table went completely silent. Mr Yılmaz's face became as red as a tomato.

Then Paşa said, in Mrs Yılmaz's voice: "Where is the cheap rice? I'm not using the good rice for them!"

The children looked under the table. Mrs Yılmaz looked at the ceiling. Mr Kaya put down his fork.

And then, slowly, Mr Kaya started to laugh. It was a big, loud laugh, and soon his wife was laughing too.

"Don't worry," said Mr Kaya. "You are right. I do talk too much. My wife says it every day."

"And the rice is delicious," said Mrs Kaya.

After that night, the two families became good friends. But when Aunt Selma came back from Australia, the Yılmaz family gave Paşa back very, very quickly.
