The Missing Key
A2

On Saturday morning, Mrs Parker could not find her key. She looked in her bag, in her coat, and under the sofa. It was not there.

"Somebody took it," she said to her neighbour, Leo. Leo was twelve years old, and he wanted to be a detective.

"Don't worry," Leo said. "I will find it."

He took a notebook and a pencil. First, he asked Mrs Parker some questions.

"When did you last see the key?"

"Last night. I came home at eight, I opened the door, and I put the key on the little table."

"Who visited you last night?"

"Nobody. Only my cat, Pepper, was here."

Leo wrote everything in his notebook. Then he looked around the room. The little table was next to the window. The window was open.

On the floor, under the window, there was a small white feather.

"Interesting," said Leo.

He went out into the garden and looked up at the big tree. High in the tree, there was a nest. Something in the nest was shining in the sun.

Leo climbed the tree carefully. In the nest, he found a silver spoon, two shiny buttons, a piece of foil, and a key.

A black and white bird was watching him from another branch. It looked very angry.

"Sorry, my friend," Leo said. "This one is not yours."

When he gave the key to Mrs Parker, she was very happy. She made hot chocolate for him and asked, "How did you know?"

Leo smiled and showed her the feather. "Magpies love shiny things," he said. "And they never close the window."

The next day, Mrs Parker bought a small box for her key. It had a lid, and it was not shiny at all.
