The Lost Wallet
A2

Rosa was walking home from school when she saw something brown on the ground. It was a wallet. She picked it up and looked inside. There were three hundred euros, two bank cards, and a small photo of a baby.

Rosa's family did not have much money. Her mother worked long hours at a hotel, and Rosa needed new shoes for winter. For a moment, she thought about the money. Then she looked at the photo of the baby again.

There was also a card with a name and an address: Mr Daniel Costa, 12 Bridge Street, Costa's Bakery.

The bakery was near the river. When Rosa walked in, the shop smelled of warm bread and cinnamon. A tall man with flour on his hands was standing behind the counter. He looked worried.

"Excuse me," Rosa said. "Are you Mr Costa? I think this is yours."

The man looked at the wallet, and then at Rosa. He opened it. Everything was there.

"I looked for this all morning," he said. "The money is for my daughter's doctor. Thank you. Thank you so much."

He wanted to give her fifty euros, but Rosa said no. "I didn't find it for money," she said.

Mr Costa thought for a moment. "Then can I give you something different? I need help in the bakery on Saturdays. Would you like a job?"

Rosa smiled. "Yes, please!"

Now Rosa works at the bakery every Saturday morning. She sells bread, cleans the tables, and is learning to make cinnamon rolls. With her first money, she bought new shoes for winter.

And on the wall behind the counter, there is a new photo: Mr Costa, his baby daughter, and Rosa, all with flour on their faces.
