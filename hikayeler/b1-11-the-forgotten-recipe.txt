The Forgotten Recipe
B1

Grandma Nur had been the best cook in the family for sixty years. Every holiday, every birthday, every Sunday, her kitchen was full of people and the smell of her famous lemon cake. She never used a recipe. "It's all in my hands," she used to say.

Then, slowly, she began to forget things. First it was small things: her keys, the name of a neighbour, whether she had taken her medicine. Then bigger things. One day, she got lost on the way back from the market she had visited every week for forty years. The doctor said it was dementia. It would get worse, he said, and there was no cure.

Her granddaughter, Aslı, was nineteen. She moved in with her grandmother to help, while she studied at the university nearby. Some days were good. Some days, Grandma Nur thought Aslı was her sister, who had died many years ago. Some days she didn't recognise anybody at all.

The thing that made Aslı saddest was the kitchen. Grandma Nur didn't cook anymore. She sat by the window and looked at the street, and when Aslı asked her about the lemon cake, she just looked confused.

Nobody in the family knew the recipe. Aslı searched through the old notebooks in the kitchen, but there was nothing. "It's all in my hands," she remembered, and she felt like crying.

One rainy afternoon, Aslı decided to try anyway. She found a basic lemon cake recipe online and started putting the ingredients on the kitchen table: flour, sugar, eggs, butter, lemons.

Grandma Nur came into the kitchen. She looked at the table for a long time. Then, without saying anything, she picked up a lemon and smelled it.

"Not this one," she said clearly. "Too hard. You need a soft one. With a thin skin."

Aslı didn't move. Her grandmother put the lemon down, chose another one, and began to grate the skin into a bowl. Her hands moved quickly and confidently, as if they belonged to a different person. She added sugar without measuring. She cracked the eggs with one hand. She told Aslı to put the butter near the oven to make it soft, "but not too soft."

Aslı grabbed a notebook and wrote down everything, as fast as she could. A handful of sugar. Three eggs, maybe four if they were small. The juice of two lemons, plus "a little bit more." A spoon of yoghurt — that was the secret nobody knew.

The cake was perfect. It tasted exactly like every holiday of Aslı's childhood.

When it was finished, Grandma Nur sat down at the table and looked at the cake, confused. "Who made this?" she asked. "It smells like my mother's kitchen."

"You did, Grandma," said Aslı, and she cut her a big piece.

Today, the recipe is in a frame on the kitchen wall, in Aslı's untidy handwriting. At the top, it says: "Grandma Nur's Lemon Cake. It's all in her hands."
